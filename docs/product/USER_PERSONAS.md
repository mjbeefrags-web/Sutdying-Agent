# User personas

## Maya — time-constrained undergrad (primary)
Second-year biology student, five courses, lectures in PDF/slide form. Needs a quick way to find explanations and remember terminology without re-reading everything. Success: can answer a course question with evidence and complete a short review between classes. Risks: poor OCR, over-trust in generated explanations, limited laptop/GPU capability.

## Andre — exam-focused learner
Final-year engineering student with a fixed exam date and uneven confidence across topics. Needs diagnostic practice, a realistic weekly plan, and progress signals that distinguish guessed answers from remembered knowledge. Success: identifies weak topics early and sees improvement on delayed checks.

## Dr. Lin — educator (future stakeholder, not MVP operator)
Wants students to study accurately without creating extra grading work. Needs clear provenance and a way to flag incorrect source interpretations. Institutional dashboards, course sharing, and educator authoring are not in Phase 1.

## Accessibility and connectivity considerations
Users may navigate by keyboard or screen reader, have vestibular/low-vision needs, use integrated graphics, or have inconsistent bandwidth. Every workflow must have a semantic non-3D path, reduced-motion support, readable contrast, and resumable uploads.
